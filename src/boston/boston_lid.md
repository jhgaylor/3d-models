# Boston Relief Lid — Assembly Notes

A lid for the top of the **UniFi Toolless Mini Rack** (U-Rack-6U-TL) that doubles as a
top-down 3D map of central Boston from OpenStreetMap data. Cambridge and the Charles
are at the top left, Back Bay and the Pru at the bottom left, Beacon Hill, the Common,
downtown and the North End in the middle, and Fort Point and the harbor on the right.
North is toward the back of the rack.

- **Scale:** 1:7000 (1 mm = 7 m). Heights are exaggerated 1.5×, so the Hancock stands
  about 52 mm tall.
- **Water** is cut all the way through as a slotted grille, and **parks** as a field of
  round holes, so the rack can still vent through the lid. About 11% of the area over
  the rack opening is open, roughly 180 cm². The harbor grille lands in the back-right
  corner.

## How it sits

The lid lies on the rack's flat top frame. Each end has a notch around the handle:
- The two corners of the notch hug the handle's legs, so the lid can't slide in any
  direction.
- Between the legs the notch is cut back an extra 10 mm, so there's room for your
  fingers to wrap the bar.

Nothing hangs below the plate. To lift the rack, take the lid off first.

## Parts

| Preset | Qty | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `tile-sw` | 1 | Front-left quarter (Back Bay, the Common) |
| `tile-se` | 1 | Front-right quarter (downtown, Fort Point, Seaport) |
| `tile-nw` | 1 | Back-left quarter (Cambridge, the Charles) |
| `tile-ne` | 1 | Back-right quarter (North End, the harbor) |
| `tiles-keys` | 1 plate | 10 bowtie keys that join the tiles |

Each tile is 249.6 × 229.9 mm and prints plate side down with no supports. It fits a
256 bed.

## Printing

- **Bambu printers: keep the tile out of the exclusion zone.** Bambu Studio blocks an area
  of roughly 18 × 28 mm at the front-left corner of the plate (it's where the nozzle wipes
  and the printer doesn't probe it when levelling). A 249.6 × 229.9 tile placed centred on
  a 256 mm bed overlaps it, and Bambu Studio warns that the object is "too close to the
  exclusion area". A corner printed over that zone also sits on an unprobed patch of the
  bed, which is the likely cause of a lifted corner on a first print. Fix: rotate the tile
  90° so the 229.9 mm side runs along X, then slide it right until its left edge is past
  X = 18 mm (the warning goes away), or just use auto-arrange, which does the same.
- **Material:** PETG (or ASA) is better than PLA. The lid sits in the rack's warm exhaust
  air and spans about 400 mm unsupported, and PLA creeps at those temperatures.
- **Layers:** 0.2 mm. Heights are rounded to 0.2 mm steps.
- **Settings:** 3 walls and 15% infill are plenty. The buildings are small, so slow the
  minimum layer time down or the tall towers will wobble.
- **Two colors (optional):** a filament change at 4.2 mm (just above the 4 mm plate)
  gives you a light ground with darker buildings.

## Assembly

1. Lay the four tiles face down on a flat surface, butted together.
2. Glue a key into each bowtie pocket across the seams. Use CA or epoxy. There are 4
   keys across the left/right seam and 6 across the front/back seam.
3. Let it cure flat. Then set it on the rack, notches over the handles.

## Regenerating the map

The map data is in `vendor/boston_map.inc`, generated by `tools/boston_map.py`:

```sh
pip install shapely
python3 tools/boston_map.py            # uses the cached download in .cache/osm/
python3 tools/boston_map.py --refresh  # re-download from Overpass
```

The centre, scale, vertical exaggeration and building-height defaults are at the top
of that script. Buildings with no `height` or `building:levels` tag get 10 m.

The script keeps neighbouring building columns 0.015 mm apart (`COL_GAP`). Columns that
share a wall produce a mesh that touches itself, which slicers report as non-manifold
edges in the STL. The gap is far below what a printer resolves. After a rebuild,
`python3 tools/check_3mf.py build/boston_lid.tile-*.3mf` should report `touching 0` for
every tile.
